# AI Research Tools Mapped by Research Workflow Stage

**Research Date:** November 2025
**Research Focus:** Companies/projects using LLMs to accelerate general research tasks, organized by research lifecycle stage

---

## Executive Summary

This research identified **40+ companies and projects** using LLMs to accelerate research across seven workflow stages. Key findings:

### Distribution Insights
- **Most Crowded Stages:**
  - **Stage 2 (Literature Review & Discovery):** Most mature market with 10+ established players
  - **Stage 6 (Writing & Communication):** Highly competitive with numerous AI writing assistants
  - **Stage 7 (Full-Cycle/Multi-Stage):** Emerging category with significant 2025 activity

- **Underserved Gaps:**
  - **Stage 3 (Experimental Design & Planning):** Few commercial offerings, mostly research projects
  - **Stage 4 (Execution & Data Collection):** Limited to specialized domains (bio labs)
  - **Stage 1 (Ideation & Hypothesis Generation):** Emerging area, mostly academic tools

- **Key Trends:**
  - Rapid shift toward full-cycle automation in 2025
  - Strong YC investment in research assistant tools
  - Growing focus on agentic, autonomous research systems
  - Open-source projects driving innovation in automation

## Key Observations & Market Analysis

### 1. Stage Concentration Patterns

**Mature Markets (High Competition):**
- Literature Review & Discovery (Stage 2): 10+ established players
- Writing & Communication (Stage 6): Numerous specialized tools
- Strong product differentiation through features (citation analysis, consensus meters, semantic search)

**Emerging Markets (Innovation Frontier):**
- Full-Cycle Automation (Stage 7): Rapid growth in 2025 with major breakthroughs
- High investment activity (Lila: $350M, multiple YC batches)
- Shift from human-in-loop to fully autonomous systems

**Underserved Markets (Opportunity Gaps):**
- Experimental Design (Stage 3): Few commercial tools, mostly academic
- Execution & Data Collection (Stage 4): Domain-specific only (bio labs)
- Ideation (Stage 1): Emerging but underdeveloped commercially

### 2. Technology Trends

**Agentic Systems Dominance:**
- Move from single-task tools to autonomous agents
- Multi-step reasoning and planning capabilities
- Progressive refinement through tree search (AI Scientist v2)

**Open Source Leading Innovation:**
- Major breakthroughs from open-source projects (Sakana, AI-Researcher)
- Commercial spinouts following research validation
- GitHub as primary distribution channel for cutting-edge tools

**Cost Efficiency Breakthrough:**
- Full research papers generated for ~$15 (AI Scientist)
- Democratization of research capabilities
- Economic viability of fully automated research

### 3. Funding & Investment Patterns

**Strong YC Presence:**
- Heavy investment in research assistant tools
- Multiple companies per stage (Undermind, AnswerThis, Anara, Sciloop, Novaflow)
- Focus on workflow automation and productivity

**Mega-Rounds for Full-Cycle:**
- Lila Sciences: $350M Series A
- Large bets on complete research automation
- Autonomous labs combining AI + robotics

**Freemium Dominance in Stage 2:**
- Many literature tools free or freemium (ResearchRabbit, Semantic Scholar, Connected Papers)
- Revenue through Pro tiers and institutional licenses

### 4. Research vs. Commercial Balance

**Academic Projects Driving Innovation:**
- Cutting-edge capabilities emerge from research (MIT CRESt, IRIS, AI Scientist)
- Commercial lag time of 1-2 years for productization

**Fast Commercial Adoption:**
- Quick commercialization paths (FutureHouse → Edison Scientific)
- YC as accelerator for research-to-product transitions

### 5. Integration Trends

**Multi-Stage Platforms Emerging:**
- Tools expanding across adjacent stages (Elicit, Paperguide, SciSpace)
- Full-stack research platforms vs. point solutions
- API integrations becoming standard (Zotero, Mendeley)

**Specialization vs. Generalization:**
- Domain-specific tools for biotech/computational biology
- General research tools spanning all domains
- Tension between depth and breadth

### 6. Timeline to Full Automation

**Industry Predictions:**
- OpenAI: Intern-level researcher by 2026, full AI scientist by 2028
- Current state (2025): Workshop-quality papers already achieved (AI Scientist v2)
- Rapid acceleration: 3 years of human progress in 2 weeks (DeepScientist)

**Bottlenecks:**
- Experimental execution still requires physical infrastructure (except computational domains)
- Quality assurance and validation
- Ethical considerations and human oversight

### 7. Geographic & Institutional Distribution

**Strong Academic-Industry Links:**
- MIT, Stanford, Berkeley leading research
- University spinouts common (FutureHouse → Edison)
- Allen Institute (AI2) driving non-profit innovation

**YC as Central Hub:**
- Significant concentration of tools in YC portfolio
- Validates market opportunity in research automation
- Network effects in research tool ecosystem
